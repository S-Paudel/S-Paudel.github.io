Personal

My journey has taken me from designing integrated pest management programs for vegetable farmers in South and Southeast Asia to leading biosecurity and invasive species research across the Pacific. Along the way, I completed a Ph.D. in Entomology and International Agriculture & Development at Penn State University, where I developed a strong interest in using science to address global challenges related to food security, sustainable agriculture, climate resilience, and international development.

I am particularly interested in turning research into real-world impact. Whether supporting biosecurity preparedness, assessing emerging pest risks, developing integrated pest management strategies, or contributing to international development initiatives, I enjoy connecting evidence with action. My work spans invasion ecology, risk assessment, surveillance, modelling, stakeholder engagement, extension, policy support, and capacity building across diverse agricultural systems.

Beyond research, I actively contribute to community organisations, governance initiatives, and conversations on equity, inclusion, and global scientific collaboration. As a scientist from Nepal working across international research and development systems, I care deeply about strengthening opportunities for diverse voices in agriculture, biosecurity, and sustainable development, while building partnerships that create lasting impact for communities and future generations.

Personal

I grew up in Nepal, where agriculture is deeply connected to livelihoods, communities, and opportunity. That perspective has shaped my career across science, development, and biosecurity. Today, as a Senior Scientist in New Zealand, I work at the intersection of invasive species management, sustainable agriculture, and international development.

My journey has taken me from designing integrated pest management programs for vegetable farmers in South and Southeast Asia to researching the invasion ecology of the coconut rhinoceros beetle across Pacific Island nations. Along the way, I completed a Ph.D. in Entomology and International Agriculture & Development at Penn State University, where I developed a strong interest in how science can address global challenges such as food security, climate resilience, and sustainable development.

I am particularly interested in turning research into real-world impact. Whether supporting biosecurity preparedness, working with stakeholders on pest management strategies, or contributing to international development initiatives, I enjoy connecting evidence with action. My work spans field ecology, modelling, risk assessment, extension, policy engagement, and capacity building across diverse agricultural systems.

Beyond research, I actively contribute to community organisations, governance initiatives, and discussions on equity in science. Having navigated international research systems as a scientist from the Global South, I care deeply about creating opportunities for diverse voices in agriculture, biosecurity, and global development.

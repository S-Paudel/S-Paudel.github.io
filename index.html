Sulav Paudel, Ph.D..

Senior Scientist | Biosecurity, Pest Management & International Development

Tracking one beetle across an ocean — I study invasive insect pests and integrated pest management across the Pacific, with roots in Nepal and a career built between smallholder fields and biosecurity labs.

01

Personal

I grew up in Nepal, where agriculture is not an abstraction — it's the work that feeds a household. That's the lens I've carried through every posting since: from coordinating smallholder programs in Kathmandu, to a PhD in entomology in Pennsylvania, to tracking an invasive beetle across the South Pacific from a lab in Lincoln, New Zealand.

I'm drawn to problems that sit between science and people — where good data only matters if it reaches a farmer, a customs officer, or a community group in time to act on it. Outside of formal research, that same instinct shows up as governance work, advocacy, and volunteering with Nepalese and biosecurity community groups (see Community).

As a scientist from the Global South working in international development, I've also written about the practical barriers — visas, travel, and access — that shape whose science gets to travel and whose doesn't. It's part of why I care about building capacity locally, not just publishing from afar.
